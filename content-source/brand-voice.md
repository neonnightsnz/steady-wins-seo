### Steady Wins Keywords That Rank

## Why This Document Exists

Every piece of copy Steady Wins publishes must hit like a freight train — intense, high-energy, and aggressively focused on turning passive website visitors into paying clients.

These guidelines cover **two distinct voices** — sales copy and client-relationship copy — plus the sub-brand variations, so nobody applies "crush the competition" energy to a review reply, and nobody promises something in a headline that support then has to walk back.

## Two Voices, Not One

Steady Wins runs on two registers, and mixing them up in the wrong place is the fastest way to sound off.

### Sales voice — landing pages, ads, gig copy, cold outreach

This is the voice the rest of this document describes: aggressive, dominant, built to convert a stranger.

### Relationship voice — review replies, order updates, direct messages, support

This is the voice that's *already working* in your gig reviews — keep it there on purpose:

> *"Pleasure working with you. Thank you for your business and trust."* *"No probs, I will send you a video explanation on this asap. Thanks again!"* *"Very patient buyer. Thank you"*

**Rule of thumb:** if the reader has already paid you, they don't need to be sold — they need to feel like a person, not a lead. Drop the "dominate the market" language entirely once someone's a client. Stay direct and clear, but warm.

|  | Sales voice | Relationship voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Stranger, hasn't paid | Existing client |
| Job | Convert | Retain, reassure |
| Example | "Stop lighting cash on fire with fluff agencies." | "Thanks for the order! I'll get the keyword report over to you by Friday." |

## Voice & Tone Principles (Sales Copy)

### 1. Unapologetic, High-Energy Sales Drive

Steady Wins doesn't beg, soft-pedal, or hide behind corporate buzzwords. We state the brutal reality of digital competition, blow up weak excuses, and offer the only strategy that actually moves the revenue needle. Speak like a top-tier growth strategist who knows *exactly* how to dominate search engines.

**Do this:**

- Attack the status quo directly
- Demand action using strong active verbs
- Frame every feature as raw pipeline growth and revenue

> *"Stop lighting cash on fire with fluff agencies that deliver 'brand awareness.' Grab real search rankings that print money."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"Our team offers comprehensive search engine optimization packages to suit your business needs."~~ ~~"We aim to improve your search presence over time."~~

---

### 2. Ruthless Problem Identification

Prospects don't buy products; they buy escape from pain. Drag their current struggles into the spotlight. Expose the hidden costs of low search rankings and force them to face what inaction is costing them daily.

**Do this:**

- Highlight lost revenue and wasted ad spend
- Call out useless SEO tactics and zero-ROI retainers
- Make staying still feel far more dangerous than taking action

> *"While your competitors steal your prospective clients daily, your site is sitting invisible on page 4 gathering dust."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"It can be hard to rank in a crowded market."~~ ~~"Low visibility might affect your overall visitor numbers."~~

---

### 3. Irresistible, "Godfather" Value Stacking

When offering a solution, don't just list features — stack value so high that turning it down feels financially irresponsible. Frame the price as a tiny drop in the bucket compared to the massive return on investment.

**Do this:**

- Stack main deliverables alongside high-value bonuses
- Explicitly calculate or showcase the total value return
- Present guarantees that are actually true (see Guardrails below) and remove risk from the buyer

> *"You get our entire Keyword Ranking Engine, a complete site audit, AND a 1-on-1 strategy call for just $499."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"Packages start at $499 per month for optimization services."~~ ~~"We strive to offer good value across all service tiers."~~

---

### 4. Artificial Scarcity & Brutal Urgency

Every offer needs a fuse attached to it. Give prospects a visceral reason to act *right now* rather than thinking about it tomorrow.

**Do this:**

- Use firm deadlines, strict client caps, and limited-time pricing — **only when real** (see Guardrails)
- Emphasize the immediate cost of waiting another day
- Force a clear, binary decision: win now or fall behind

> *"This discounted intake window shuts down Friday night. Lock in your rate before your competitors claim your market share."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"Feel free to get in touch whenever you are ready to start."~~ ~~"Our special rate will be available for a short time."~~

---

### 5. Concrete ROI & Brutal Specificity

Vague claims lead to dead conversions. Numbers, timeframes, exact mechanics, and hard metrics prove authority. Tell prospects *exactly* what they get, *exactly* how much it costs, and *exactly* what outcome to expect — and only claims you can actually back up on delivery.

**Do this:**

- Use exact dollar amounts, precise percentages, and concrete metrics
- Outline exact deliverables instead of general services
- Show exact steps from sign-up to execution

> *"We don't sell 'SEO tasks.' We map 50 high-intent transactional search terms, audit your top 3 competitors, and engineer your path to Page 1."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"We pick great keywords for your business."~~ ~~"Our service delivers lots of traffic."~~

### The rule

Don't say **"effective tactics"** when you can say **"hyper-targeted, high-intent transactional keywords."**

Don't say **"affordable pricing"** when you can say **"start for just $499/mo and claim a 10% discount on Month 1."**

Don't say **"satisfied clients"** when you can state exact performance wins and client revenue numbers — but only ones you can point to.

## Guardrails: What We Never Claim

This is what separates "hits hard" from "gets disputed." Every guardrail below traces back to a real point of confusion in past client feedback.

- **Never promise a specific rank or timeframe** ("guaranteed #1," "rank in 7 days") unless the package literally contracts to that. "Guaranteed satisfaction" and "guaranteed #1 on Google" are not the same claim — don't let copy blur them.
- **Always make recurring vs. one-time unmistakable.** A past client was surprised a package was monthly, not a single delivery — the hero copy and the fine print must agree. If it's a subscription, say "monthly" in the headline or tagline, not just the price line.
- **"Unlimited traffic" needs a boundary somewhere on the page.** Aggressive copy can say it in the headline, but the deliverables section should state the actual mechanism (keyword count, link volume, content cadence) so the claim is figurative, not literal.
- **Scarcity must be real.** If client intake isn't actually capped, don't say "we can only accept 5 clients this month." Fabricated scarcity is the fastest way to erode trust with the exact audience sharp enough to notice.
- **Don't imply results are typical if they're your best case.** If a stat comes from one standout client, say so ("one client saw...") rather than presenting it as the expected outcome.

None of this softens the voice. "You get 40 links from vetted authority sites, priced per link, delivered in 3 weeks" is still aggressive copy — it's just aggressive copy that's also true.

## Voice in Different Situations

### Delivering a limitation or boundary

**Do this:**

> *"We don't take on every business that applies. If your offer isn't validated or your site is broken beyond repair, we'll tell you straight."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"We regret to inform you that we cannot accept all client applications at this time."~~

### Pitching against competitors

**Do this:**

> *"Traditional agencies waste months writing 'monthly reports' while your rankings flatline. We target the terms your buyers are actively searching for right now."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"Unlike other agencies, we focus heavily on quality keyword selection."~~

### Setting the terms of an offer

**Do this:**

> *"Grab 10% off your first month, a full SEO Audit, and a strategy session — but only if you lock in your spot before the countdown timer hits zero."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"Offers are subject to availability and terms apply."~~

### Replying to a review or client message (relationship voice)

**Do this:**

> *"Thank you! Great working with you — let me know if you need anything else on the report."*

**Not this:**

> ~~"Thank you for choosing Steady Wins to dominate your market."~~ (too much — this is a person, not a landing page)

## Vocabulary

Use sharp, aggressive, conversion-focused words in sales copy. If it sounds like an academic paper or a polite email, burn it — unless you're writing the relationship voice, where "polite" is correct.

**Words that fit Steady Wins (sales copy):**

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Domination | crush, dominate, claim, conquer, obliterate, outrank |
| Growth | scale, profit, explosive, pipeline, revenue, conversion |
| Urgency | lock in, claim now, strike, immediately, window closing |

**Words to avoid (sales copy):**

- Weak passive terms: *help, try, hope, strive, consider*
- Corporate fluff: *synergy, holistic approach, brand journey*
- Defensive language: *fairly good, reasonable results, modest gains*

**Words to avoid everywhere, including sales copy (see Guardrails):**

- Unqualified absolutes: *guaranteed #1, guaranteed rank, unlimited* (without a stated mechanism), *instant*

## Tone

Steady Wins sales copy feels:

- High-energy and relentless
- Direct, bold, and authoritative
- Unapologetic about selling high-value solutions
- Fast-paced and urgent

**Avoid anything that feels (in sales copy):**

- Passive, tentative, or overly polite
- Corporate, bureaucratic, or academic
- Apologetic or timid

*(Relationship voice inverts several of these on purpose — see "Two Voices" above.)*

---

## Cadence & Rhythm

Short, aggressive statements. Punchy flow. Rapid-fire value delivery.

> *"Target the terms. Claim the traffic. Own the market."*

**Write like this:**

- Single-line punchy paragraphs
- High use of action verbs
- Direct, uncompromising calls to action

**Avoid:**

- Long, winded academic paragraphs
- Passive voice
- Weak, indirect sentence structures

## A Quick Gut Check

**Before publishing sales copy, ask:**

- Does this grab the reader by the collar in the first 3 seconds?
- Is the pain of inaction clearly stated?
- Is the value stack absurdly compelling?
- Is there a clear reason to take action *today*?
- Does it sound like an undisputed industry authority?
- **Is every specific claim in here something we can actually deliver on?**

**Before sending a client/review reply, ask:**

- Does this sound like a person, not a landing page?
- Would I be embarrassed if this showed up on the sales page instead?

If yes to the right list — launch it.
